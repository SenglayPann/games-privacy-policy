Privacy Policy Peaks & Perils

Last updated: 24 October 2026

This privacy policy explains what information the game Peaks & Perils: TriPeaks (the “game”) collects, why, who it is shared with, and the choices you have. The game is made and published on Google Play by Cyteoqz (Pann Senglay, Phnom Penh, Cambodia), called “we” below.

Contact for any privacy question or request: cyteoqz@gmail.com

The short version

  • You do not need an account, and we never ask for your name, email, phone number, contacts, photos or location.
  • Your progress (runs, records, settings) is saved on your phone. If you use Google Play Games, a copy is also kept in your Google account so it can come back on another phone, and your achievements and leaderboard scores go to Google Play Games, where other players may see them (see section 1a).
  • With your permission, the game sends anonymous play statistics so we can make the game better. You can say no, and change your mind any time in Settings. If the game crashes, a technical crash report is sent so we can fix it.
  • The game shows ads. The ad companies collect some device information to show and measure ads (see below). You choose whether ads may be tailored to you: in Europe and the UK the game asks first, and everywhere Settings has a “Tailored ads” switch.
  • Purchases go through Google Play. We never see your card or payment details.

1. Saved on your phone

The game saves your progress, records and settings in its own storage on your phone. We cannot see it. Apart from the copy kept with Google Play Games (section 1a), it does not leave your phone. Uninstalling the game, or clearing its storage, deletes the copy on your phone. Android may also back up the game’s storage to your Google account and put it back when you reinstall the game or set up a new phone (Android’s own backup, which you control in your phone’s settings).

1a. Google Play Games (optional)

The game uses Google Play Games, a service of Google, if you are signed in to it on your phone. The game signs in by itself when your phone is already signed in, and otherwise asks once whether you want to; you can play without it. When you use it:

  • A copy of your progress (the same information as in section 1: Trail Marks, Summits, records, charms, settings, the Daily Peaks you played) is kept in your Google account (Play Games’ “Saved Games”), so it can come back on another phone or after a reinstall, and so the game can tell when today’s Daily was already played on another phone. Google shows it with a short line (your highest Summit and Trail Marks), a picture and the time played.
  • Achievements you earn are sent to Google Play Games.
  • Your scores go to the game’s leaderboards (Daily Peaks, this week’s Dailies, the Summit ladder), each with a short tag the game reads back: your highest Summit, how many Dailies you played, your favourite charm and the title you wear. Other players of the game can see your scores, tags, Play Games name and picture on these leaderboards, as Google allows: Google lists only players whose Play Games activity is public, and shows friends’ scores only to friends.
  • Your Play Games name and picture are shown in the game (on your profile, the lobby and the share picture), and the names and pictures of other players on the leaderboards. If you allow it in Google’s own question, the game also reads your list of Play Games friends to show their scores.

Google handles this information under its own privacy policy (https://policies.google.com/privacy) and your Play Games settings (for example, who can see your game activity). We do not receive your email address or your Google password.

2. Play statistics (only with your permission)

On first launch the game asks whether you agree to send play statistics. If you agree, the game uses Unity Analytics, a service of Unity Technologies, to send:

  • How the game is played: for example when a run or a hand starts and ends, its score, which charms were offered and picked, and which settings were changed;
  • A random ID made for this installation (not your name, and not linked to your Google account);
  • Device and app information: phone model, operating system version, game version, language;
  • Approximate location: the country or region, worked out from your internet connection (never your precise location);
  • Purchase events: that the Peaks Pass was bought (never payment details).

We use this only to understand how the game is played and find where it is too hard or confusing. If you say no, none of this is sent. You can change your answer in Settings (Play stats) at any time.

2a. Crash reports

If the game crashes, freezes or hits an error, Unity Diagnostics (Unity Technologies) sends a technical report: the error, the phone model, the operating system and game versions, and performance information. We use it only to find and fix the problem. It does not include your name or contact details; if you said no to play statistics, Unity leaves out the identifiers it would otherwise use for analytics.

3. Ads

The game is free and shows ads. Some are optional: you choose to watch one for a reward. Ads are delivered by Unity LevelPlay (Unity Technologies, formerly ironSource), which may work with other ad networks. To show, limit and measure ads, and to prevent fraud, these companies collect:

  • your device’s advertising ID and other device identifiers;
  • approximate location (from your internet connection);
  • how you interact with ads (for example, that an ad was shown or tapped);
  • device and performance information.

Tailored or plain ads. Tailored ads are chosen using what the ad companies know about your device; plain ads are not. In the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland (as your phone’s SIM, network, region or time zone shows), the game asks at its first start whether ads may be tailored to you, and loads no ad before you answer; saying no is as easy as saying yes. Everywhere, Settings → “Tailored ads” turns them off or on at any time. Turned off, the game tells the ad companies that you did not consent to tailored ads and that your information must not be sold or shared for advertising (as US state privacy laws provide). You see as many ads either way. You can also reset or delete your advertising ID, or opt out of personalized ads, in your phone’s settings (Settings → Google → Ads, or Settings → Privacy → Ads).

The ad companies handle this information under their own privacy policies: Unity (including LevelPlay and Unity Ads): https://unity.com/legal/privacy-policy

4. Purchases

The Peaks Pass is bought through Google Play. Google handles the payment under its own privacy policy (https://policies.google.com/privacy). We receive only a confirmation that the purchase happened, so the game can unlock it. The game remembers on your phone whether you own the Pass, and asks Google Play again at each start, so a refund removes it and a reinstall gives it back.

5. Who we share information with

Only the service providers named above (Unity Technologies and its ad partners, and Google for purchases and for Google Play Games), for the purposes above. With Google Play Games, other players of the game can see what section 1a says. We do not sell your personal information, and we do not share it with anyone else unless the law requires it.

6. How information is protected and kept

Information sent by the game is encrypted in transit (HTTPS). We do not run our own servers: play statistics and crash reports are stored by Unity, and we keep them only as long as they help us improve the game. The copy of your progress, your achievements and your scores in Google Play Games are kept by Google in your Google account until you delete them (section 7). Ad data is kept by the ad companies under their policies.

7. Your choices and requests

  • Play statistics: turn them off any time in Settings (Play stats).
  • Tailored ads: turn them off or on any time in Settings (Tailored ads); see section 3.
  • Delete your data: see Delete your data below.
  • Google Play Games: to remove the game’s copy of your progress, its achievements and its scores from your Google account, delete the game’s data in Google Play Games (Google’s steps: https://support.google.com/googleplay/answer/9130646). The game keeps its own copy on each phone where it is installed and sends it to Google Play Games again at its next start, so first uninstall the game, or clear its storage, on each of your phones. Who can see your game activity is set in your Play Games settings.
  • Questions or complaints: write to cyteoqz@gmail.com. We answer within 30 days. Depending on where you live, you may also have the right to complain to your local data protection authority.

Delete your data

To delete the play statistics Peaks & Perils sent from your phone:

  1. Open Peaks & Perils and tap Settings.
  2. Tap “Delete my data” and confirm.

The game stops sending statistics and asks Unity to delete every statistic linked to your installation’s random ID. Unity deletes them within 30 days. What is kept: crash reports, which Unity keeps under its own privacy policy. If you have already uninstalled the game, we cannot find your statistics, because they are linked only to a random ID and not to you; Unity deletes raw play statistics after 13 months. An email cannot be matched to an ID, so writing to us is for questions only. For ad data, you can also reset your advertising ID in your phone’s settings. Your progress in Google Play Games: see section 7.

8. Children

The game is made for adults and is not directed to children under 13. We do not knowingly collect information from children. If you believe a child has used the game and information about them was sent, write to us and we will ask for it to be deleted.

9. Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, we will publish the new version at this address with a new effective date. If a change is important, the game will also tell you.
